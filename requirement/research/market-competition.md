## DoNotPay Precedent

- FTC settlement September 2024: **$193,000 fine** for claiming AI could substitute for human lawyers without testing or employing lawyers
- Required consumer notifications to subscribers
- Prohibited from making unsubstantiated capability claims
- Sources: ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/donotpay; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoNotPay

## SRL Market Size

- **30–40% of Family Court of Australia matters** involve self-represented litigants at some point (Productivity Commission, Access to Justice Arrangements)
- Very few Australian jurisdictions keep detailed SRL statistics (IAJ-UIM, Self-Represented Litigants in Australia, 2025)
- SRLs skew lower income, lower legal literacy

## Corporate Legal Team Procurement Requirements

From Minter Ellison (major AU law firm) AI procurement guidance (26 Aug 2025):
- AI procurement must be risk-classified (high/low/exempt)
- High-risk (legal decision-making, personal data) requires: independent audits, training data documentation, ongoing monitoring, human oversight
- Contracts must include: data ownership, liability, transparency, human oversight clauses
- Verify vendor governance, audit trails, certifications (e.g., ISO/IEC 42001)
- Questions to ask: What data trained the AI? Will user data train the model for others? Does it comply with privacy/discrimination laws? Who owns IP? How is liability managed? Are there audit trails?

Source: minterellison.com/articles/procuring-ai-key-considerations-and-strategies
