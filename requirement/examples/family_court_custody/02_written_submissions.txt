### I. INTRODUCTION

This submission is made in the capacity of Plaintiff, [Your Name], who has been involved with this family matter since May 2026 and seeks to establish a clear legal framework for our children's upbringing based on their best interests under Australian Family Law principles (FLA).

The core issue at hand involves the distribution of parental responsibility regarding medical decisions, schooling arrangements, and daily living logistics. While both parties have agreed that an equal shared arrangement is fair, this submission argues that such a division has been fundamentally displaced by significant long-term issues: the applicant's inability to provide consistent therapeutic support for their son R (a child with speech-delay diagnosis), while simultaneously seeking sole responsibility on major medical matters pending future therapy outcomes.

The respondent seeks 50/50 shared parental responsibility from Term 1 of this maintenance order, which ignores the practical and emotional realities that have existed since separation in May 2026. The applicant proposes a modified arrangement: equal non-medical responsibilities with sole responsibility for medical decisions pending R's therapy review, followed by an assessment on whether to shift to six or eight nights per fortnight upon completion of this Term.

### II. FACTUAL BACKGROUND AND CURRENT STATUS (May – Present)

**1. The Nature of the Relationship and Logistical Reality**
Since May 2026, both parties have lived with their respective children for a total of five nights in each fortnight. This arrangement has been established to ensure continuity of care during periods when one parent is absent or unavailable due to work commitments.

*   **The Applicant's Role:** The applicant was the primary carer and guardian since 2019, providing daily supervision, meal preparation (where appropriate), and emotional support for both children A (Year 7) and R (preschool).
*   **R's Needs:** Since February 2026, R has been diagnosed with speech-delay. The applicant alone has managed all aspects of therapy: weekly appointments with the school counsellor, daily supervision at home, medication management, and communication regarding health updates for both children.

**2. The Respondent's Position (50/50 from Term 1)**
The respondent proposes a standard equal shared arrangement based on their current capacity to co-decide without significant disruption to the therapeutic relationship or medical stability of R. This position ignores:
*   **Therapeutic Continuity:** For five consecutive fortnights, R has received consistent therapy under one parent's care alone; shifting this responsibility would likely disrupt his progress and cause anxiety for both children.
*   **Medical Stability:** Major medical decisions regarding treatment plans are critical to the well-being of a child with speech-delay. The applicant is uniquely positioned to manage these complex health issues without compromising R's therapeutic relationship or safety.

**3. Current Legal Status**
No family violence orders have been on foot, and there are no allegations of substance abuse by either party regarding their children. Both parents reside within 6km of the school where both children attend (A is Year 7; R is preschool). The applicant has demonstrated a proven ability to provide consistent care since May 2019 without any complaints from the other parent or third parties.

### III. LEGAL ARGUMENT AND BEST INTERESTS ANALYSIS

Under Section 60 of the Family Law Act (FLA), the court must determine whether an order is in "the best interests" of the child(s). The applicant submits that while equal shared parental responsibility may be a desirable outcome, it cannot stand alone when there are overriding factors favoring sole decision-making for medical matters pending therapy outcomes.

#### A. Consideration 1: Best Interests (Section 60CC)
The primary considerations under Section 60(2)(a) of the FLA include "the child's welfare." The applicant argues that a shared arrangement ignores this consideration because it fails to protect R from harm while simultaneously failing to provide meaningful therapeutic support.

*   **Therapeutic Continuity:** A parent must be able to maintain consistent relationships with their children, especially those who require specific therapies (e.g., speech therapy). To shift responsibility solely on medical grounds would likely cause significant distress for both children and disrupt the ongoing therapeutic relationship R has built over five consecutive fortnights.
*   **Protection from Harm:** The respondent's proposed arrangement ignores a critical safety factor: ensuring that major health decisions are made by someone who can best manage them without compromising the child's emotional or physical well-being, particularly given R's speech-delay diagnosis.

#### B. Consideration 2: Best Interests (Section 60CC(3))
The applicant submits that Section 60(3) requires consideration of "the practical difficulty and expense" involved in making decisions for the child(s). The respondent fails to adequately consider this factor when proposing a standard equal split based on their current capacity.

*   **Logistical Difficulty:** Both parents reside within 6km of R's school (A is Year 7; R is preschool). This proximity makes it extremely difficult and expensive for one parent to manage the logistical complexities of schooling, including:
    *   Coordinating with teachers regarding attendance or special needs.
    *   Managing daily routines without disrupting a child who requires consistent supervision at home.
*   **Cost:** The respondent's proposed arrangement implies that both parents must share significant costs associated with maintaining this level of care and managing the school logistics, which is not sustainable given their current living arrangements (6km from school).

#### C. Consideration 3: Effect on R Given a Fresh Diagnosis
The applicant argues that introducing change into an already stable therapeutic relationship for five consecutive fortnights could have adverse effects on R's development. A fresh diagnosis of speech-delay, combined with the logistical burden mentioned above, creates a high risk of disruption to therapy and potential anxiety in both children.

### IV. THE PLAINTIFF'S PROPOSED MODIFIED ARRANGEMENTS

To address these best interests considerations while maintaining fairness, the applicant proposes the following terms for Term 1:

**A. Shared Non-Medical Responsibilities (50/50)**
The respondent agrees that both parents will share equal responsibility regarding non-medical decisions such as school attendance arrangements, daily living logistics, and general household management where feasible within their respective capacities. This ensures a level of care for the children without imposing undue burden on either party who may be unable to manage all these tasks alone due to distance or time constraints.

**B. Sole Responsibility Pending Therapy Review (Medical)**
The applicant proposes that **sole parental responsibility shall remain with the Applicant until R's speech therapy outcome is reviewed.** This includes:
*   Major medical decisions regarding treatment plans, medication management, and health assessments for both children A and R.
*   Any changes to care arrangements necessitated by a new diagnosis or significant change in therapeutic needs.

**C. Staggered Move to Six-Night Arrangement (Conditional)**
The applicant proposes that upon the completion of Term 1 review:
1.  The court will consider whether it is beneficial for R's therapy relationship and medical stability to be maintained by a single parent per fortnight.
2.  If positive, the arrangement may transition from five nights/fortnight (current) to six or eight nights depending on the specific needs of each child post-review.

### V. RESPONDENT'S POSITION AND REASONING FOR DENIAL

The respondent argues that:
1.  **Equity and Fairness:** A standard equal split is a fair reflection of their current capacity, which allows them to manage day-to-day logistics without significant disruption or cost for either party. The applicant's sole responsibility on medical matters ignores the practical difficulty mentioned above.
2.  **Therapeutic Impact:** Shifting this burden solely based on medical status disregards five consecutive fortnights of consistent therapeutic support provided by one parent alone, which is essential for a child with speech-delay.

### VI. CONCLUSION AND REQUESTS

The applicant submits that the respondent's position ignores fundamental best interests considerations regarding:
*   **Therapeutic Continuity:** The need to maintain R's relationship and therapy without disrupting his progress over five consecutive fortnights.
*   **Logistical Burden:** The practical difficulty of managing schooling logistics for a child in preschool when both parents reside within 6km, which is not sustainable under the respondent's current arrangement.

The applicant requests that this matter be treated as distinct from any maintenance order currently pending regarding R's medical care or therapy continuity. Specifically:
1.  **Denial** of equal shared parental responsibility for non-medical decisions on Term 1.
2.  **Granting Sole Responsibility** with the Applicant until R's speech therapy outcome is reviewed (Section 60CC(3)).
3.  A conditional move to six or eight nights per fortnight upon successful completion of this review, provided that therapeutic continuity remains intact for both children.

The applicant asserts that their current arrangement has been proven effective in providing consistent care and support without any allegations of violence or abuse by either parent. Therefore, the court should not substitute a different legal framework based on capacity alone when there are overriding factors favoring sole decision-making pending therapy outcomes.
